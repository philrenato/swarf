every word in swarf every named thing, one line each

operations

rough
the first pass: it clears most of the wood in flat layers above the part, fast and deep, and leaves a little for contour to finish. its step-down stays under half the bit's width; its stepover is 40–60% of the bit.
contour
smooth surface pass after rough. the cutter crawls across the X or Y axis at a fine stepover, riding the part's curves. typical stepover: 5–15% of tool diameter on finishing.
outline
cuts the part free from the board along its outside edge. usually the last step. leave tabs so the part doesn't fly out.
pocket
clears flat-bottomed hollows: trays, recesses, a sunk hole. pick the floor face and swarf clears down to it, inside its walls. a flat part has no floor to pick: the card's pocket in tex button opens tex with inlay picked, where you drag the shape and type the depth.

parameters

stepover
how far the bit moves sideways between passes, as a share of its width (the step card's % of bit). larger = faster but rougher; smaller = smoother but slower. roughing 40–60%, finishing 5–15%.
step-down (depth of cut)
how deep each pass cuts, in mm or inches as mm | in says. swarf keeps every preset under half the bit's width: in wood that leaves the flutes room to clear the chips.
feed rate
how fast the bit moves through the wood, in mm/min (in/min when mm | in says in). the material, the bit and the step-down all affect it. when in doubt: slower is safer.
plunge rate
how fast the tool drives down into material. usually 30–50% of feed rate. too fast and you snap end-mills.
spindle speed
RPM of the bit. a bigger bit turns slower. on the ShopBot you set it on the router's dial, 1 (10,000 rpm) to 5 (21,000), and the cut card names the setting.
ease down
ramp into the cut at an angle instead of plunging straight down. easier on the tool, especially for end-mills with no center cut. on by default in swarf.
depth first
finish each Z layer top-to-bottom in one region before moving to the next. opposite is layer-first (sweep all regions per layer).
tab
a small uncut bridge of stock that holds the part in place during the outline cut, so it doesn't shift or fly out. break or sand off after.
auto tabs
tabs › auto tabs puts a starting set of tabs on the selected part at once, sized for its material, the outline's bit, the board's thickness and the machine (undo or remove all takes them back). how many: fewer to more. size: smaller to larger. each is an ordinary tab: click one to pick it, then drag it along the edge, press delete, or change its length, height and reach in its card. one you moved or resized stays as you set it when the tabs are placed again (back to automatic lets go), and where you deleted one stays empty; placing again never touches tabs you placed with tabs › +. every hole that leaves a loose piece (wider than two bits and 3 mm) gets tabs too, so nothing cut free can catch the bit and fly; hold inside pieces turns that off. with no tabs, concerns and control's cut checklist say what would come loose; the concern's own button (auto tabs, or hold inside pieces for a loose piece) places them on that part, one undo. the line above the button says how many tabs are on the part now: 0 in amber means none yet.
tab settings
every number in tabs is a field: length, height, reach (across the cut), how many, from corners, hold inside pieces on or off, holes from (how wide a hole is before it gets tabs), and how many in each hole. an empty field is automatic and shows its number in gray; type one (3/4, 12 mm) to pin it, empty it to go back. a part's tabs follow its settings: change a field and they move at once; scale the part, or change its material, bit or machine, and they are placed again. length, height and reach also resize the tabs you placed by hand. all automatic empties every field. remove all takes every tab away.
leave
on an outline step: wood the first passes leave on the part. 0 leaves none. with a last pass it comes off at the end; without one the part stays that much oversize.
last pass
on an outline step: one more time round at the whole depth, at the true size, for a clean edge. leave about 1/2 mm for it. tabs stay in both passes: a part with no tabs would be skimmed loose.
stock
the board on the machine's bed. swarf sizes it to the parts; advanced lets you size it yourself.
z top / z bottom
upper and lower Z bounds for the carving. defines the slab swarf will work inside.
z clearance
how high above stock the tool retracts during rapids. high enough to clear clamps; low enough not to waste time.

tools

end-mill
flat-bottomed cutter, the workhorse. diameter and flute count vary; fewer flutes = better chip clearance for soft material.
ball-end
spherical tip. for 3D contour finishing on curved surfaces. leaves a cusp pattern proportional to stepover.
v-bit
conical cutter for engraving and chamfering. depth controls the line width.
drill
plunges straight down to make holes: add a step › drill finds the round holes that are the bit's size, drawn or modeled, and pecks down to the bottom of the board. a hole bigger than the bit needs an outline or a pocket.

machines

Langmuir MR-1
benchtop CNC mill, 30×17×16" envelope, BT30 spindle. it takes the exported file.
ShopBot Basic
4 × 8 ft bed. a Porter-Cable 7518 router: swarf switches it on and off, you set its speed on its dial (1–5). driven from swarf by control.

materials (starting defaults)

hardwood
oak, maple, walnut, cherry — sharp HSS leaves a clean edge. dust mask. feed, plunge, spindle, step-down per tool diameter live in swarf-materials.json.
plywood
birch or Baltic ply, the shop's usual board. cuts like hard wood; its glue lines dull a cutter faster and the top veneer can tear out: a sharp bit, the good face up. OSB and hardboard (masonite) have their own presets.
foam
rigid PU, EPS, blue/pink insulation. fastest cuts in the library; main risk is tool deflection on long flutes.
aluminum 6061
MR-1 only: the ShopBot cuts no metal. chips should curl, not powder. keep RPM as low as the spindle allows. a mist of WD-40 or alcohol helps.
polycarbonate
Lexan and similar. gummy — melts if you feed too slow. single-flute O-flutes are ideal; 2-flute HSS works if chips eject.
mild steel
MR-1 only. occasional only. HSS on prosumer CNC is slow and punishing. small stepdowns, lots of lubricant, listen for chatter and back off.

the ShopBot at KCAD

permission
the ShopBot runs with a faculty member or the technician's say-so. ask before you start.
your board
bring your own board, flat and labeled with your name, with extra for a test cut. 1/4 to 2 1/2 in thick, up to 4 × 8 ft; thinner sheet goes to the laser.
your drawing
closed outlines only, and no doubled lines on top of each other: swarf cuts every line it is given.
protection
safety glasses and hearing protection on, dust collection on, hair and sleeves back.
the floor e-stop
the red button on the floor, pressed with your foot. in an emergency, step on it and keep clear until everything has stopped.
hours
the ShopBot runs when the woodshop is open; check its hours.

making and running

control
drives the ShopBot from swarf over its USB cable, in Chrome or Edge only. on Windows, quit ShopBot 3 first: the box takes one program at a time, so press disconnect (or close the swarf tab) before you go back to ShopBot 3. jog, zero, trace, air cut, cut. only shows when the ShopBot is the machine; the MR-1 uses export. the top row says what the buttons move: the practice simulator, the ShopBot, or no machine. the jog panel says the next thing to do.
windows laptop
on the shop PC, ShopBot's own software already has the box's driver: quit ShopBot 3 and press connect. on your own Windows laptop (no ShopBot software), give the box a driver once: download Zadig (zadig.akeo.ie), plug in the box, choose Options › List All Devices, pick V204ShopBot (USB ID 10C4 83C4), set the driver to WinUSB and press Replace Driver. then press connect in Chrome or Edge. never do this on a PC that runs ShopBot 3: it stops ShopBot 3 reaching the box.
inches
tap mm | in at the top left (or setup › preferences › units), and swarf's shape, size and draw cards and control's jog show inches. type 3/4, 1 1/2, 0.75 or 19 mm: a length with its unit works in either setting. control runs .sbp files written in inches (VCarve's ShopBot post): the checklist shows the cut's size in inches and mm for you to confirm. a file made with Z zero on the table is still refused. a fraction with no unit is inches even in mm (3/4 is 19.05 mm), and swarf says so; for part of a millimeter type 0.75 or 3/4 mm.
the shop's bits
the tool list has the woodshop's ShopBot bits by their part numbers: 1/4 straight 48-005, 1/4 up-cut 52-910, 1/4 down-cut 57-910, 1/2 straight 48-072. an up-cut lifts its chips out: it clears a deep cut best but can fray the top face. a down-cut pushes the top fibers down for a clean top face, and swarf keeps its passes to half its diameter so the chips it packs into the slot don't burn. in wood, if swarf's own speed or pass for one of these bits would run more than half again above the shop's numbers (120 in/min and 1/8 in a pass for the 1/4 in ones), swarf uses the shop's. check the bit's length before a deep cut: swarf takes 1 in until the maker's numbers are in.
trace / air cut / cut
each one opens a checklist first. trace runs the outline of the cut, router off; an air cut runs the whole file above the board; cut starts the router. a button that is waiting tells you what it still needs.
jog
opens on continuous at 1000 mm/min (40 in/min): hold an arrow or a button and it moves until you let go; no end stops on this machine, so let go in time. pick a step and each press moves that far. go-to buttons and home ask first. space or esc stops; while a job runs, any key pauses it.
table corners
no home switches on this machine: put the bit over the table's front-left corner once and swarf learns it until the machine is switched off. then zero at, or go to, the front-left or front-right corner.
z plate
finds the top of the board by touching the bit to a metal plate. put the plate and its clip away before you cut.
board thickness
after the z plate touches, its card asks (if you like) for your board's thickness as you measured it with calipers. the machine cannot tell: it knows the top of the board, not the spoilboard. more than 1 mm from the job's thickness and the card says what follows: the cut stops short of going through, or goes into the spoilboard. it never stops you and never moves the machine.
router
Porter-Cable 7518. swarf only switches it on and off; set its speed on its dial, 1 (10,000 rpm) to 5 (21,000).
test cut
learn the machine on scrap plywood. the square and the circle: a groove you measure with calipers. cut out a square: a part held by tabs.
draw
the bed seen from above. pen, rectangle, ellipse, text; move points; give it a thickness and a top edge. done or switching mode keeps your changes; cancel drops them.
holes
in draw, an outline drawn wholly inside a part is a hole in it. the card's inside or over a part makes the next outline add to the part, cut from it, or a part of its own instead.
regions
draw › regions (G): overlapping outlines split into every region they make. click a region to fill or empty it; apply, done or another tool makes the filled regions the parts.
pencil
draw › pencil (N): drag freehand and it becomes a few smooth points that move like the pen's. points in the card fits it again: fewer is smoother. end near the start to close it.
carve, pocket or texture
three jobs, three places: words carved into a board are draw's text tool; a pocket is a step, or tex's inlay when the part is flat; a texture is tex. right-click the part and pick carve words, pocket a shape or texture an area: the right one opens with the part picked. carve words is there for a drawn or 2D part (draw can only carve into those). pocket a shape adds a pocket step when the part has a floor to pick (then open its card, press select, click the floor); a flat part opens tex to drag the shape and type its depth.
carving words
to carve words or any closed drawing into a board: draw › text, type the words, click on the board. the card reads carve into and the board's name; pick the 90° V-bit or the 60° V-bit by name, and press done. a v-carve step is added before the outline. the words stay words: open draw again to change them. a word can also be a hole, joined, cut, or a part of its own, in the same card. the pen, pencil, rectangle and ellipse carve the same way: pick carve into the part before you draw.
v-carve
a V-bit follows the middle of the shape, going as deep as the shape is wide: depth is the distance to the outline over the tangent of half the bit's angle, so the corners come out sharp. the 90° V-bit is wider and shallower, the 60° narrower and deeper. it never goes deeper than the bit's cone, the flat depth you type, or the board; past that the floor is flat, and takes longer to clear. made in draw (carve into a part) or in tex (fill › v-carve on anything painted). cut a test first: the feeds are the material's, for the bit's width at one pass.
engrave
draw a line over a part (the pencil, or the pen: click its last point again), give it an engrave depth, press done: an engrave step cuts along the line, the bit's center on it, that deep from the part's top.
tex
mark a part, then texture the mark (draw makes shapes; tex marks them). the brush (B) paints a mark, erase (U) or option takes it away, rectangle (R) and ellipse (E) drag one out, region (G) fills the part's top less its holes, points (H) reshape its edge; make it a part, or a hole, hands its outline to draw. a mark stays on the part's top and runs full to its edges. the card picks the pattern, its scale, turn, soft edge and depth, the bit and the path. done keeps the marks: each is a texture step before the outline.
fonts
draw › text › font: the built-in Russo One, this computer's fonts (Chrome and Edge ask first), or a .ttf or .otf file. each word keeps its own font.
copies
edit › copies, or right-click a part › copies: how many across, how many back, and the gap between them (12 mm to start). the count includes the part you have. they stay on the bed: the card says if they run off it or land on another part, and make waits until they fit. tabs and marks come with each copy. one undo takes them all back.
fit to part
view › fit to part, or F: brings the parts into view (in simulate, the board). a part sized or brought in so small that it would be under a fifth of the view is framed by itself.
bits
steps › bits lists the bits this machine has: name, width, kind and part number, with the V-bits first, marked for carving. the list only reads: add a bit at its foot gives the list a new flat, ball or drill bit. with view › advanced on, edit in tool library changes or removes one, and adds a V-bit.
simulate speed
simulate's speed × runs it faster or slower, and the words beside it say how that compares with the real cut: about 30× real time means a 3 min job plays in 6 s. the view frames the board beside the steps panel, looking down; F frames it again.
new
file › new starts a new job: it asks first, then every part, step and toolpath comes off the bed. your bits, the material and the machine stay. not while the machine runs or toolpaths are being made.
shape
a circle, rectangle, ring, polygon, star or slot, with a thickness.
modes
cam, draw, tex and control. draw makes shapes, tex marks and textures them, control runs the machine. in draw, tex or control the step row folds to those four.
zero at
where X 0, Y 0 sits on the stock: its front-left corner (the default), or its center for something round. you put the bit on that spot before you cut.
skin
view › skin: wet plastic (default), classic, swarf.

simulation

chips
simulate › chips, in the swarf skin (view › skin): the cutter throws chips along its spin with a kick in the feed direction: long curls, flakes, tight coils and fine grit, tinted by the material. they settle on the bed and stay there. import a new part to clear the floor.
phase aura
the whole scene gently tints to the current workflow step — neutral when idle, warm while toolpathing, cool while previewing, hot mill-red while simulating, gray after export. touch anything and the color returns.
phase LED
a small dot next to the renato.design watermark. dim = idle, amber = toolpaths, gold = preview, glowing mill-red = simulate, gray = exported. always on, always quiet.
sky
procedural two-layer mackerel sky with slow drift and camera-orbit parallax. when swarf is working (toolpaths, simulate) the clouds speed up. the colors follow the skin.
